Gapless albums on Cast are a receiver problem, not a hardware one

Live albums, DJ mixes and classical movements are split into tracks that are meant to flow into each other. On Cast speakers, music apps put a gap or a click at every split. This page plays one continuous recording, cut into 14 tracks, in three ways. The last one is seamless on a 2015 Chromecast Audio, in speaker groups too, and only the receiver's lowest layer changes (nothing changes in how the music service encodes or serves its files, or in the apps and devices: just the small JavaScript receiver page the speaker loads).

Pick a mode and play. You can switch modes while it plays, and it carries on from the same spot. The quickest test is the jump to join 10 → 11, which sits inside a rising crescendo.

    02 What you're hearing
    
    
    The three modes differ only in what happens in the last few milliseconds of each track.
    
    
    
      
    
        
    Per-track
    
        
        
    The track ends, the player tears down its stream, loads the next file and starts again. That's tens of milliseconds of silence in a desktop browser, and more on a Cast device.
    
      
    
      
    
        
    Gapless queue
    
        
        
    Tracks go back to back into one buffer, so there's no reload. But nothing removes the encoder's silent lead-in and padding, so a few milliseconds go missing or overlap. Cast devices also click where the buffer is spliced.
    
      
    
      
    
        
    Sample-accurate
    
        
        
    Each track is decoded, its lead-in and padding are cut off by sample count, and its first sample is placed right after the previous track's last one. The receiver hears one continuous waveform, exactly as it was recorded.

    03 Why the last few milliseconds are hard
    
    
    Every separately encoded Opus track carries a little extra audio that isn't part of the music:
    
    
    
      
    
    
    
      
      
    • The file says exactly how much to drop: the pre-skip sits in the header, and the padding is a field on the last block. Desktop browsers honour some of this for a single file, but not across a queue.
      • 
      
    • MSE positions tracks with millisecond timestamps. At 48 kHz that can't line up two tracks to the sample.
      • 
      
    • Cast devices decode through their own media pipeline, which clicks wherever a compressed stream is spliced, even with perfect timestamps.
      • 
    
    
  
    

  
    
    
    04 The fix: swap one SourceBuffer
    
    
    The player above it stays as it is. It keeps appending Opus into what it thinks is an ordinary SourceBuffer, keeps its queue, buffering and seeking. Only the layer that hands audio to the device changes.
